# Design Rationale

## Problem framing

The homepage needs to support two related tasks: understanding a featured offer and finding a suitable lease. When decorative content, offer conditions, selection controls, and supporting links compete equally, visitors must determine the hierarchy themselves.

## Information hierarchy

The redesign separates the experience into five layers:

1. Contact and availability information
2. Primary navigation
3. Page purpose and instructions
4. Featured offer and vehicle search
5. Supporting benefits, contact links, and legal information

## Featured offer

The dark navy surface distinguishes the offer from the surrounding page. Price and contract information remain close to the vehicle name so users can evaluate the complete offer as a group.

## Lease search

All filters are placed in one form. Visible labels explain what each choice controls, while consistent field styling and spacing make the form predictable.

## Responsive behavior

At wide desktop sizes, the offer and search form appear together for comparison. At 1,100 pixels and below, they stack into one reading sequence. Primary navigation moves into a drawer with visible open and close states.

## Visual system

- Navy establishes structure and contrast.
- Pink identifies primary actions and selected states.
- Yellow highlights the featured offer and interactive contact states.
- Blue connects the redesign to the existing brand identity.
- Light neutral surfaces prioritize readability around the search task.

## Validation needed

The next iteration should evaluate whether visitors can identify the monthly price and terms, select the correct lease type, use the mobile menu, and find contact or legal information without assistance.
